\begin{abstract}
\normalsize
How much can linearly many pairwise moment constraints reduce the best
expected coverage attainable with prescribed marginals? For every integer
\(k\geq1\), we determine the exact ratio supremum over both \(k\)-colorable
graphs and graphs of maximum degree at most \(k-1\): it is
\(\bigl[1-(1-1/k)^k\bigr]^{-1}\). The same suprema hold when each tested
joint probability may exceed the product of its marginals by any fixed
multiplicative factor.

Tightness uses disjoint \(k\)-cliques with \(n(k-1)/2\) tested pairs.
A pointwise collision certificate controls every feasible coupling while
leaving all cross-clique pairs unrestricted. It also yields heterogeneous
and additive error bounds. For the matching witness we give exact finite
optima and necessary and sufficient error scales for its limiting gap;
five small examples have rational primal and dual certificates. Finally,
we derive a finite full \(t\)-wise independence bound and an explicit rate
to the previously known \(e/(e-1)\) limit. The argument combines known
color-resampling and Cartesian-coverage ingredients with a certificate
that survives deletion of the cross-block moment constraints.
\end{abstract}
