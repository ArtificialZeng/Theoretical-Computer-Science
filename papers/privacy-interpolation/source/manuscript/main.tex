\begin{abstract}
\normalsize
We study positive probability paths whose coordinate log derivatives are bounded by a privacy budget. For every swapped triplet $(c,a,b)$ and $(c,b,a)$, with $0<a<b$ and $c=1-a-b>0$, we determine the minimum feasible budget $T_*$ and the smallest possible peak of the unchanged-endpoint coordinate at every budget $L\ge T_*$. The peak is $\max\{c,1-2a e^{L/2}\}$, attained by an explicit path; the minimum-budget path is unique. We also determine the sharp cost of requiring every coordinate to be monotone, or to remain between its endpoint values. At endpoint privacy distance $\eta>0$, the worst ratio to the unrestricted optimum is $2\log((e^\eta+1)/2)/\eta$ in every dimension at least three, with positive attaining examples, whereas the binary ratio is one. For general endpoints we give necessary excursion certificates evaluable exactly by a rational breakpoint sweep and one square root per coordinate.
\end{abstract}
