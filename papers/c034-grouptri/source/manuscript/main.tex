\begin{abstract}
For every fixed integer $M\ge2$, we give a deterministic algorithm for
Exact Triangle over compactly presented finite abelian groups of exponent
dividing $M$ and polynomial order. On a complete tripartite graph with at
most $n$ vertices in each part and group order at most $n^c$, the algorithm
runs in $O_{M,c}(n^{3-\delta_M}\operatorname{polylog}(n+2))$ word-RAM
operations, where $\delta_M>0$ is independent of $c$. This resolves the
bounded-exponent question in Majdoddin's Open Problem~21.12, using his
elementary-group algorithm as the base case. One conservative saving is
$\delta_M=10^{-3}(144000)^{1-\Om(M)}$, where $\Om$ counts prime factors
with multiplicity. We construct quotient-zero pieces using capped witness
lists and the known hitting-set/frequency decomposition. Each piece has
edgewise vertex potentials. Lifting these potentials cancels carries before
division into the kernel, even for non-split extensions. Separate absence
markers make every recursive query a legitimate complete input. All witness
acquisition, graph construction, coordinate arithmetic and recursive calls
are charged.
\end{abstract}

fine-grained algorithms, quotient and kernel}

\section{The result and its context}

Exact Triangle asks whether three edge labels around a triangle sum to zero.
The algebra of the labels matters when the group is given by short coordinate
vectors rather than by an enumerated multiplication table. We consider a
complete tripartite graph with parts $U,X,V$, each of size at most $n$.
Every edge has a label in
\[
 G=\prod_{j=1}^d\Z_{m_j},\qquad m_j\ge2,\quad m_j\mid M,
 \qquad |G|\le n^c.
\]
The presentation lists the cyclic factors and each label's residues. Both
$M\ge2$ and $c>0$ are fixed, but the algorithm must handle every dimension
allowed by this order bound. A solution is a triple whose three labels sum
to zero in $G$. We allow the empty product, representing the trivial group.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For each fixed $M\ge2$ there is a deterministic uniform word-RAM algorithm
that solves this problem, for every fixed $c>0$, in
\[
 O_{M,c}\bigl(n^{3-\delta_M}\operatorname{polylog}(n+2)\bigr),
 \qquad
 \delta_M=\frac1{1000}\left(\frac1{144000}\right)^{\Om(M)-1}.
\]
The saving is independent of $c$. The bound includes reading the input,
preprocessing, constructing every instance, coordinate arithmetic, and all
recursive calls. Words have $\Theta(\log(n+2))$ bits. Each group vector has
$O_c(\log(n+2))$ residues, and operations are charged coordinate by
coordinate.
\end{theorem}

Majdoddin~\cite{Maj26}, Theorem~21.3 and Corollary~21.7(a), supplies the
elementary-group case. His Remark~21.8 explains the difficulty in transferring
the list-based lifting method to triangles: an edge's lift carry depends on
the other labels, and a useful split must survive re-gauging. His
Propositions~21.9 and~21.10 treat, respectively, few quotient-zero triangles
and limited incident residue diversity. Open Problem~21.12 asks for a truly
subcubic algorithm without these promises, including the unrestricted
$\Z_4^d$ case and the general fixed bounded-exponent case. Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
extends the elementary backend to every fixed bounded exponent for compact
cyclic-product inputs.

Our contribution is the fully charged quotient/kernel extension step.
Chan, Vassilevska Williams and Xu~\cite[Theorem~5.1]{CVX23} give a
zero-triangle decomposition with a hitting-set/frequency structure. Its
initial witness sorting is cubic; its faster update bound cannot be used
as fresh-input preprocessing. We reconstruct the part of that structure
needed here using capped detection, and explicitly expose the edgewise
potential equations already underlying their construction. Those equations
let us cancel arbitrary lift carries before making kernel queries. We pay
for complete, explicitly labelled graphs rather than using the cost of a
compact representation. A finite induction then handles every fixed $M$.
Capped listing, hitting sets and frequency decomposition are established
supporting tools. For prior relationships between detection, all-edge
detection and listing, see Sheffield, Vassilevska Williams and Xi~\cite{SWX26}.
Our extension uses the conservative capped routine proved below.

The result removes the elementary/non-elementary distinction for this entire
bounded-exponent class, including dense quotient-zero inputs with many
incident residue classes. The quantitative guarantee is weak:
$\delta_4=1/144000000$, and it deteriorates with $\Om(M)$. The comparison
above uses the stated versioned primary results.

\section{Representation and the elementary backend}\label{sec:backend}

Throughout, $\Ot$ suppresses a fixed power of $\log(n+2)$ and constants
depending on the fixed parameters. Group coordinates have bounded modulus
when $M$ is fixed. The order promise gives $d\le c\log_2 n$ for nontrivial
inputs. Thus coordinatewise operations and comparisons cost $O_{M,c}(\log(n+2))$,
not one operation. Sorting means deterministic lexicographic sorting of
representations, without assuming an order compatible with group addition.

The embedding $\Z_m\hookrightarrow\Z_M$, $x\mapsto(M/m)x$, is injective
when $m\mid M$. Applying it to every coordinate preserves zero sums and
takes $O_{M,c}(n^2\log(n+2))$ operations. The ambient group becomes $\Z_M^d$,
of order at most $n^{c\log_2 M}$. There is no group enumeration. We may
therefore prove the algorithm for homogeneous groups.

We use the following precise prior dependency, with $\eps=1/1000$.
For every fixed prime $p$, elementary-group Exact Triangle over $\F_p^D$
has a deterministic algorithm taking
\begin{equation}\label{eq:backend}
 O_{p,C}\bigl(b^{3-\eps}\operatorname{polylog}(b+2)\bigr)
 \quad\text{whenever }p^D\le b^C,
\end{equation}
for every fixed $C$, with the saving independent of $C$.
Indeed, Majdoddin~\cite[Theorem~21.3]{Maj26} states the running time
\[
 O\bigl(p^6b^{3-\varepsilon\gamma}(\log b)^6\bigr)
\]
under his matrix primitive
$H(\varepsilon,\gamma)$. His Corollary~9.1(b) and Section~16 instantiate
$H(1/14,0.077)$, giving $\varepsilon\gamma=0.0055>\eps$;
Corollary~21.7(a) makes this unconditional. For a fixed $p$, the restriction
$p\le b^{\varepsilon/4}$ excludes only a finite range, handled by direct
enumeration. We rely on this stated prior theorem and its matrix dependency,
rather than assuming a new matrix oracle. The algorithm uses the actual
dimension, not a supplied numerical bound $C$.

For the rest of the proof, write the labels on $U_iX_k$, $X_kV_j$, $U_iV_j$
as $A_{ik},B_{kj},-C_{ij}$, respectively. We seek
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero}
 A_{ik}+B_{kj}-C_{ij}=0\quad\text{in }\Z_M^d.
\end{equation}
Missing edges arise only inside reductions; the original input is complete.
Here is a uniform way to represent them. Over any $\Z_q^D$ with $q\ge2$,
append three coordinates, one for each edge kind. Present edges have zero
markers. An absent edge has zero original coordinates and marker $1$ in its
own kind's coordinate, zero in the other two. These are the actual
\emph{plus-sum} labels. A triangle has one edge of each kind, so its marker
sum is zero exactly when all three edges are present. The construction
works also in characteristic two, where one shared sentinel could cancel.
Encoding a graph of side $b$ costs $O(b^2(D+3))$ operations.

\section{Capped witnesses from legal detection queries}\label{sec:capped}

The following standard detection/listing construction records the cutoff
needed when dimension is measured against the original input size.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:capped}
Fix a prime $p$, constants $c>0$ and $0<\theta<1$, and a positive integer
cap $T=O(n^{1-\theta})$. On a possibly masked instance over $\F_p^d$ with
$p^d\le n^c$, one can deterministically obtain, for each outer pair $(i,j)$,
exactly $\min(t_{ij},T)$ distinct middle witnesses, where $t_{ij}$ is its
true number of present zero triangles. The fully charged time is
$\Ot(n^{3-\eps\theta/6})$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Handle $n\le1$ directly. Pad the three parts to the least power of two
$N\ge n$, marking every edge incident to a padded vertex absent. Put
$\alpha=\theta/6$, and let $b_0$ be the least power of two at least
$N^\alpha$. Thus $N^\alpha\le b_0<2N^\alpha$, except for the harmless
equality at $N=1$. Store a counter and a list for each original outer pair.

Traverse the product octree of the three padded intervals in depth-first
order: each cube of side $b>b_0$ has eight children of side $b/2$. On
entering a cube, construct a fresh complete encoded query. Its active edges
are the original present edges, except that any outer pair whose counter
has reached $T$ is also absent. Query~\eqref{eq:backend}, using the three
absence coordinates. A negative cube returns. A positive cube of side
larger than $b_0$ visits its children. At a positive cube of side $b_0$,
scan every triple, outputting exactly the genuine present zero triangles
whose pair is still unsaturated; increment its counter immediately.

Every emitted witness is genuine, and leaves have disjoint tuple domains,
so witnesses are distinct. Every positive cube emits at least once before
returning. To see this, choose an active witness at entry. If no earlier
descendant emits, its pair remains unsaturated and its path stays positive
until a leaf emits it. If an earlier descendant emits, the assertion already
holds. Depth-first execution ensures that no outside subtree changes the
counters during this argument. A pair finally below $T$ never saturated,
so none of its witnesses could have been pruned; all were emitted. A pair
at $T$ has exactly $T$ distinct stored witnesses. This proves the cap claim.

All queries have $b\ge b_0\ge N^\alpha$. Since $n\ge2$,
\[
 p^{d+3}\le p^3n^c\le N^{c+3\log_2 p}
       \le b^{(c+3\log_2 p)/\alpha}.
\]
Thus one fixed order exponent makes every detector call legal. There are no
queries below this cutoff. Word sizes $\log b$ and $\log n$ differ by a
fixed factor. At side $b$ there are at most $(N/b)^3$ queries. Both their
construction and detection cost at most
$\Ot(N^3b^{-\eps})$, since $b^2\le b^{3-\eps}$. Summing over levels
gives $\Ot(n^{3-\alpha\eps})$, including negative queries and control.

There are $Z\le n^2T=O(n^{3-\theta})$ outputs. Distinct positive leaves
emit distinct tuples, so at most $Z$ leaves are scanned. Their total cost
is $\Ot(Zb_0^3)=\Ot(n^{3-\theta+3\alpha})
=\Ot(n^{3-\theta/2})$. This is dominated by the detector bound. Counters,
lists, padding and coordinate operations fit the same bound; cubes can be
streamed. If $b_0=N$, the scan concerns a finite range depending on $\alpha$,
or follows the same estimates. No preprocessing universe is assumed.
\end{proof}

\section{A charged quotient decomposition}\label{sec:pieces}

Let $M$ be composite, choose its least prime divisor $p$, and put $q=M/p$.
Reduce $A,B,C$ modulo $p$, writing $a,b,c$ for labels in $Q=\F_p^d$.
Choose a rational $0<\theta<1$, to be fixed in Section~\ref{sec:cost}, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:params}
 s=2^{\lfloor\theta\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\rfloor},\qquad
 L=\lfloor n/s\rfloor,\quad T=L+1,\quad r=s^2.
\end{equation}
Here $s=\Theta(n^\theta)$, $1\le s\le n$, and $T=O(n^{1-\theta})$.
Integer logarithms and rational arithmetic implement these parameters
without floating-point rounding. Define
$W_{ij}=\{k:a_{ik}+b_{kj}=c_{ij}\}$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:capped}. A pair with fewer than $T$ stored witnesses
is \emph{light}; its list is complete and has size at most $L$. Put these
triangles in $R_{\rm few}$. All other pairs are \emph{heavy}. For each
heavy pair retain its genuine stored set $S_{ij}$ of size $L+1>n/s$.
The total number of stored incidences is $O(n^3/s)$.

Greedily hit these sets by middle vertices. If $m$ unhit sets remain,
their total incidences exceed $mn/s$, so some vertex hits more than $m/s$
sets. Repeatedly choose a vertex of maximum current incidence degree.
After $O(s\log(n+2))$ choices all sets are hit; denote the chosen set by
$H$. Assign each heavy pair its first chosen hitter $h_{ij}\in S_{ij}$.
In particular, this pivot is a real witness, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pivot}
 c_{ij}=a_{i h_{ij}}+b_{h_{ij}j}.
\end{equation}
For $s=1$ no pair is heavy, since $T=n+1$.
To implement the greedy rule, use incidence lists and a max heap of degrees,
breaking ties by vertex index. Once a set is hit, delete its incidences and
update degrees. Each incidence is deleted once; setup, updates and pivot
assignment take $O((n^3/s+n^2+n)\log(n+2))$ operations.

For each $h\in H$ and $k\in X$, sort the $|V|$ values $b_{hj}-b_{kj}$,
keeping the list of indices for each value. Let $F_{hk}$ contain the values
of frequency strictly greater than $n/r$. There are at most $r$ such values;
give them a canonical lexicographic order. Write $t_{hk\ell}$ for the
$\ell$-th one, when defined, with $1\le\ell\le r$. All tables take
$\Ot(|H|n^2)=\Ot(sn^2)$ time and storage.

For each $(h,i,k)$ look up $v=a_{ik}-a_{ih}$. If $v\notin F_{hk}$,
scan its index list, of size at most $n/r$, and retain a triple $(i,k,j)$
only if $(i,j)$ is heavy and $h_{ij}=h$. Put these triples in $R_{\rm low}$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:pivot} and the matched value show they are quotient-zero.
The assignment filter prevents duplicates from different pivots. The total
scan and setup cost is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowcost}
 \Ot(|H|n^2+|H|n^3/r)=\Ot(sn^2+n^3/s).
\end{equation}

For each $(h,\ell)\in H\times\{1,\ldots,r\}$ form a masked graph $P_{h\ell}$
on the original vertex sets. Its present edges are exactly
\begin{align}
 U_iV_j &: (i,j)\text{ is heavy and }h_{ij}=h,\nonumber\\
 U_iX_k &: t_{hk\ell}\text{ is defined and }a_{ik}-a_{ih}=t_{hk\ell},\label{eq:pieces}\\
 X_kV_j &: t_{hk\ell}\text{ is defined and }b_{hj}-b_{kj}=t_{hk\ell}.\nonumber
\end{align}
There are $|H|r=\Ot(s^3)$ pieces.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:partition}
The quotient-zero triangles are the disjoint union of $R_{\rm few}$,
$R_{\rm low}$, and the triangles of all $P_{h\ell}$.
On a fixed piece there are potentials $\alpha_i,\beta_j,\tau_k\in Q$
such that every present edge satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:potentials}
 a_{ik}=\alpha_i+\tau_k,\qquad
 b_{kj}=\beta_j-\tau_k,\qquad
 c_{ij}=\alpha_i+\beta_j.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A light pair contributes precisely its listed quotient-zero triangles.
For a heavy quotient-zero triangle, its assigned $h=h_{ij}$ is unique,
and~\eqref{eq:pivot} gives
$a_{ik}-a_{ih}=b_{hj}-b_{kj}$. If this value is low frequency, the scan
puts it in $R_{\rm low}$. Otherwise it is in a unique slot $\ell$ of
$F_{hk}$, and all three edges satisfy~\eqref{eq:pieces}. Conversely, a
triangle in a piece is quotient-zero by the same equations. Uniqueness of
the assigned pivot and of the slot for fixed $(h,k)$ proves disjointness.

For a fixed piece, take $\alpha_i=a_{ih}$, $\beta_j=b_{hj}$, and
$\tau_k=t_{hk\ell}$ if defined, otherwise $\tau_k=0$.
The two incident-edge identities follow from~\eqref{eq:pieces}; the outer
identity follows from the assigned actual witness~\eqref{eq:pivot}.
If the slot is undefined, both incident edge kinds at $X_k$ are absent,
so its arbitrary potential makes no assertion about a present edge.
\end{proof}

This is the hitting/frequency mechanism of~\cite[Theorem~5.1]{CVX23},
reconstructed over $Q$ using only equality and subtraction. What changes
in its construction is the capped acquisition of genuine witnesses, rather
than cubic initial sorting. Materializing all pieces still costs
$\Ot(s^3n^2)$; that charge is essential below.

\section{Removing lift carries and encoding the kernel}\label{sec:kernel}

Fix $P_{h\ell}$ and its potentials in Lemma~\ref{lem:partition}.
Lift $\alpha_i,\beta_j,\tau_k$ to vectors $u_i,v_j,z_k\in\Z_M^d$
by choosing coordinate representatives in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$.
These lifts need not define a homomorphism. On present edges put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjust}
 A'_{ik}=A_{ik}-u_i-z_k,\qquad
 B'_{kj}=B_{kj}-v_j+z_k,\qquad
 C'_{ij}=C_{ij}-u_i-v_j.
\end{equation}
Each vector reduces to zero modulo $p$ by~\eqref{eq:potentials}, so it
belongs to $(p\Z_M)^d$. On any present triangle the lifted potentials
cancel exactly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cancel}
 A'_{ik}+B'_{kj}-C'_{ij}=A_{ik}+B_{kj}-C_{ij}\quad\text{in }\Z_M^d.
\end{equation}
The additive map $p\Z_M\longrightarrow\Z_q$, $px\mapsto x\bmod q$,
is well-defined and bijective: two representatives $px,py$ agree modulo
$M=pq$ exactly when $x=y\bmod q$. This works also when $p\mid q$.
Consequently dividing all three adjusted vectors by $p$ turns their
zero-sum condition into the original condition~\eqref{eq:zero}.
There is no assumption that the extension splits and no discarded carry.

Make a complete kernel instance over $\Z_q^{d+3}$. A present edge gets
plus-sum label $A'/p$, $B'/p$, or $-C'/p$, with zero markers. An absent
edge gets zero original coordinates and $1$ in its own edge-kind marker.
The marker argument in Section~\ref{sec:backend} proves that every zero
triangle of this complete instance uses present edges. Together with
\eqref{eq:cancel}, this gives an exact correspondence between its zero
triangles and the original zero triangles contained in the piece.
In particular the marker on the third edge is $+1$ as a plus-sum label;
the sign convention for $C$ does not alter the absence test.

Each query has the same vertex-side bound $n$ as the original instance,
and $q^{d+3}\le n^C$ for a fixed $C$ depending on $M,c$, for $n\ge2$.
For example, the extra constant factor $q^3$ is at most
$n^{3\log_2 q}$. Along recursion only three coordinates per layer are
added, and the depth depends only on $M$. Thus the inductive order promise
is valid in every call. We do not retain a large dimension while applying
the kernel algorithm to tiny sides. All edge tests, potentials, adjusted
coordinates, divisions, and markers take $\Ot(n^2)$ time per piece;
this is an explicit complete graph, not an oracle representation.

\section{The algorithm and its total cost}\label{sec:cost}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main} by induction on $M$. Empty parts return NO.
If the input group is trivial, a nonempty complete graph returns YES.
For $n\le1$, read and check its at most one triangle directly. Apply the
coordinate embedding of Section~\ref{sec:backend} otherwise. For prime $M$,
use~\eqref{eq:backend}, setting $\delta_M=\eps$.

For composite $M$, let $p$ be its least prime divisor, $q=M/p\ge2$, and
use the already established algorithm at exponent $q$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 \theta=\delta_q/12,\qquad \alpha=\theta/6,\qquad
 \delta_M=\eps\delta_q/144=\delta_q/144000.
\end{equation}
Construct the capped lists, exception sets and pieces of
Section~\ref{sec:pieces}. Check every exception in the original group,
returning YES if it satisfies~\eqref{eq:zero}. For each piece construct the
complete kernel instance of Section~\ref{sec:kernel} and run the exponent-$q$
algorithm. Return YES if any call does, and NO otherwise.

Correctness follows from the disjoint partition in Lemma~\ref{lem:partition}
and the kernel correspondence. Every original zero triangle is quotient-zero,
so it is checked directly or represented in a kernel query. Conversely,
exception checks are made in the original group, and a positive kernel query
has no absent edge and satisfies~\eqref{eq:cancel}. All recursive decisions
are correct by induction. Only decision is needed from the kernel algorithm.

For clarity the full ledger, including every complete graph construction, is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Operation & Charged time, up to fixed polylogarithms\\
\midrule
Capped quotient witnesses & $n^{3-\eps\theta/6}$\\
Lists, hitting set, light exceptions & $n^3/s+n^2$\\
Frequency tables, low exceptions & $sn^2+n^3/s$\\
All materialized kernel graphs & $s^3n^2$\\
All recursive kernel calls & $s^3n^{3-\delta_q}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The $\log(n+2)$ factors in $|H|$ and sorting are included in the notation.
In particular we multiply the entire inductive cost by the number of pieces;
there is no amortized free call or uncharged initialization. Input reading,
coordinate embedding, direct exception tests, control, and termination are
covered by the displayed terms. Streaming one complete piece at a time is
sufficient. Stored witness lists, frequency tables, counters and incidence
structures have polynomial size, so their indices occupy a constant number
of logarithmic words. No unit-cost long integer operations are used.

Because $s=\Theta(n^\theta)$, the possible exponent savings in this ledger
are at least
\[
 \eps\theta/6,\quad\theta,\quad1-\theta,\quad
 1-3\theta,\quad\delta_q-3\theta=3\delta_q/4.
\]
Here $0<\delta_q\le\eps\le1$, so the smallest is
$\eps\delta_q/72$. Taking half of it in~\eqref{eq:recurrence} leaves
slack to absorb every fixed polylogarithm, even when its exponent or constant
depends on $M,c$. All rounding in~\eqref{eq:params} changes only fixed
multiplicative constants. Fixed-prime exceptional ranges of the backend,
and any finite small-$n$ cases, are absorbed by direct enumeration with
constants depending on the fixed parameters. This proves the stated time
bound. Iterating~\eqref{eq:recurrence} until a prime remains gives
$\delta_M=10^{-3}(144000)^{1-\Om(M)}$, independently of $c$.

Uniformity here means an explicit deterministic procedure for the compact
input: the dimension is read, the least prime divisor and rational parameters
are computable from $M$, and every loop and recursive call is specified.
There is no advice depending on $n$, no preprocessed universe, and no supplied
order exponent required by the algorithm. All recursive exponent parameters
are smaller, so termination follows as well. This completes the proof.

\section{Verification and limitations}

The theorem is a decision algorithm on complete compact inputs for fixed
$M$, with an explicit saving depending on $M$. Its proof uses the stated
elementary-group theorem of~\cite{Maj26} and the existing matrix primitive.
The source's full chain of matrix dependencies is used as prior work; a
formal verification of that chain is outside this paper.

Exact Python checks supplement the argument. A separately written verifier
checked 512 capped-listing instances on sides in $\{0,1,2,3\}$ with varying
caps and traversal orders; 676,333 one-coordinate potential/kernel cases
for composite $M\le36$ and proper prime divisors; and 3,548,440 marker cases
for $2\le q\le36$. It also checked 360 seeded graph decompositions with
sides $1$--$6$ and dimensions $1$--$3$, yielding 6,850 explicit kernel
instances, and 2,047 exact rational exponent ledgers for $2\le M\le2048$.
The graph tests include ordinary and dense quotient-gauge inputs. Their
detectors are brute force; these are finite semantic checks, neither a
benchmark of the fast algorithm nor a proof of the infinite theorem.
The supplied verification scripts and their coverage records are suitable
for reproducing these finite checks locally.
