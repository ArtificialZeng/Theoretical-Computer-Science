\begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}
Given a sequence of $n$ real values and $m$ rectangles in index--value space,
we give a uniform deterministic algorithm reporting a longest strictly
increasing subsequence in every rectangle in
$O((n+m)n^{2/3}\log^{30}(n+m+2))+O(K)$ operations, where $K$ is the total
output length. The result allows repeated values, arbitrary overlaps and all
answer lengths. It answers the deterministic subquadratic existence question
posed by Karthik C.~S. and Saladi Rahul for uncolored two-dimensional reporting,
while remaining slower than their randomized square-root bound.

The main step implements deterministic relative sampling for an implicit
family of canonical longest chains. A trace on selected points must be
computed through unselected original points; recomputing a longest chain on
the selected points gives the wrong range space. We compute exact compressed
source trees on original index intervals and charge their construction over
disjoint intervals in merge/reduce. Signed descendant sums evaluate every
conditional score, and upward-rounded integer probability tables give a
finite-word deterministic compression. The resulting hitting set combines
with truncated-LIS and endpoint-stitching routines, expanding only final
witnesses. Generic relative sampling, merge/reduce, conditional expectation
and the reporting framework are established ingredients; the contribution
is their background-sensitive implementation and full charged integration.

\section{The problem and the algorithmic advance}
For real values $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, identify position $i$ with the point
$(i,a_i)$. A query $Q=[\ell,r]\times[a,b]$ asks for a longest sequence
$i_1<\cdots<i_h$ of points in $Q$ with
$a_{i_1}<\cdots<a_{i_h}$. The output consists of the positions themselves.
Let $K$ be the sum of the reported lengths. Input-value comparisons and
operations on $\Theta(\log(n+m+2))$-bit words have unit cost; arithmetic on
more words is charged. No arithmetic on the real values is needed.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $n\geq1$, every $m\geq0$, every real sequence of length $n$ and
every family of $m$ index--value rectangles, a uniform deterministic
algorithm reports an optimal strict increasing subsequence in each rectangle
using
\[
 O\bigl((n+m)n^{2/3}\log^{30}(n+m+2)\bigr)+O(K)
\]
comparison and word operations. The constants are universal.
\end{theorem}

In particular, $m=\Theta(n)$ gives truly subquadratic overhead. Empty
answers, ties, arbitrary overlaps and every optimum length are included.
The exponent $30$ is a conservative envelope for the explicit costs below.
Karthik C.~S. and Saladi Rahul~\cite{KR26}, Theorems 1 and 5 and Section 7
of the full version~\cite{KRfull}, give randomized square-root-overhead
two-dimensional reporting, a deterministic truncated-LIS routine, and the
deterministic subquadratic question. We retain their short/long decomposition:
solve short answers directly and hit a canonical optimum of every long
query with a small stitching set. Our exponent is larger than their
randomized exponent; the advance is a deterministic full-model algorithm.

The sampling rate itself is established. Relative approximation bounds
and deterministic constructions appear in~\cite{HS11}, Theorem 2.11 and
Lemma 2.15 of its preprint. Deterministic merge/reduce and subsystem oracles
appear in~\cite{BCEG04}, Section 2. For canonical chains, the selected trace
depends on original points outside the selected set. Thus those generic
results do not provide a free subset-only oracle. We implement an oracle
costing $O(|I|s\log^2 n+s^2)$ for $s$ selected occurrences in an original
index interval $I$. Disjoint original intervals at each merge level make
that extra background cost affordable. Compressed-tree score aggregation
and finite-word compression complete the implementation.

Consistent-path covers and explicit-family path hitting are prior
tools~\cite{TSP11,ACC19}; neither small-cover existence nor conditional
expectation is claimed as a separate contribution. Strong deterministic
index-interval LIS results~\cite{GGK25,Kor26} concern subarrays, without an
independent value interval filtering every internal point. We make no
improvement claim for that one-dimensional interface. The bounded literature
comparison does not certify exhaustive priority.

The research and exposition used substantial OpenAI Codex assistance for
proof development, literature comparison, exact diagnostic code and writing;
the final methodology statement specifies its scope. The finite computations
are diagnostics, and the proof below supplies the universal argument.

\section{Canonical paths and the full-background trace oracle}
Write $u\prec v$ if $u<v$ and $a_u<a_v$. For comparable endpoints let
$P(u,v)$ be a longest chain from $u$ to $v$, breaking ties by the
lexicographically least sequence of original indices. Include singletons
$P(u,u)$ and the empty chain; call this global family $\mathcal F$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:consistency}
Every subpath of $P(u,v)$ between vertices $x,y$ is $P(x,y)$. For a multiset
$S$ of $s$ selected occurrences, every nonempty trace of $\mathcal F$ on
$S$ is the trace of $P(x,y)$ for two selected vertices. There are at most
$d^2$ such descriptors, where $d\leq s$ counts distinct real vertices.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Replacing a subpath by a longer chain improves the entire length; replacing
it by an equal-length lexicographically earlier chain improves its tie order.
Each replacement stays feasible because its internal points lie strictly
between the two endpoints in both coordinates. This proves consistency.
For a nonempty trace take its first and last distinct selected vertices
$x,y$. Consistency gives the asserted trace. All occurrences of a repeated
vertex belong together. Dummy occurrences belong to no path. Empty traces
remain empty under subsequent draws and require no descriptor.
\end{proof}

A rectangle optimum with endpoints $u,v$ can be replaced by $P(u,v)$:
every point of that path lies in the endpoints' coordinate box, hence in the
query. The replacement has at least the optimum length and is therefore
optimal. Hitting all members of $\mathcal F$ of length at least $t$ suffices
for every query with optimum at least $t$.

For context this family has VC-dimension at most two. If three index-ordered
points $x,y,z$ lie on a canonical path, every canonical path containing
$x,z$ contains $P(x,z)$ and hence $y$. The triple and its $\{x,z\}$ subset
cannot both be traces. If no path contains the triple, it cannot be shattered
either. This standard observation gives no efficient oracle by itself.

Suppose the real selected vertices lie in a contiguous original index
interval $I$. Every feasible path between them stays inside $I$. Computing
canonical paths using \emph{all original points of $I$} therefore gives the
global paths. Computing them on the selected set alone is wrong: for values
$(0,3,1,2,4)$, $P(1,5)=(1,3,4,5)$ has trace $\{1,5\}$ on
$\{1,2,5\}$, whereas the selected-only longest path is $(1,2,5)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:oracle}
All trace descriptors on $S$ can be represented by compressed source trees,
with occurrence counts and constant-time membership tests, in
$O(|I|s\log^2(n+2)+s^2)$ operations and
$O(|I|\log(n+2)+s^2)$ working words.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Give equal real values equal ranks by comparison sorting. For each distinct
selected source $u$, sweep original positions from $u$ rightward in $I$.
Initially only $u$ is reachable. A Fenwick structure queries ranks strictly
smaller than the current rank and chooses a predecessor with maximum
source-path length, then lexicographically least source path. Append the
current vertex to the chosen predecessor. Induction over the sweep computes
$P(u,v)$ for every reachable $v$: all feasible predecessor choices are
considered, and appending a common endpoint preserves equal-length order.

The predecessor pointers form a rooted tree. A parent always precedes its
child, so maintain binary lifting as vertices are inserted. To compare two
equal-length root paths, find their lowest common ancestor and compare the
indices of the children immediately below it. These are the first unequal
entries. This costs $O(\log(n+2))$ per comparison, making the sweep cost
$O(|I|\log^2(n+2))$ per source, including initialization and sorting. No
path scans, probabilistic hashes or unbounded lexicographic integers occur.

Mark selected vertices. In index order record the nearest selected ancestor
of each reachable original vertex, including itself when selected. For a
selected $v\ne u$, its compressed parent is the nearest selected ancestor
of its original parent. The root-to-$v$ path in this compressed tree is
exactly the selected trace of $P(u,v)$. Discard the original source tree
after compression; keep one at-most-$d$-vertex tree per selected source.
Each reachable pair $(u,v)$ is a descriptor, including $(u,u)$.

If $w_x$ is the occurrence multiplicity of selected vertex $x$, a traversal
adds $w_x$ along each compressed root path, giving its input occurrence
count. Euler entry/exit labels test whether a selected $x$ is an ancestor
of the descriptor target in constant time; this is exactly trace membership.
A dummy is never a member. The compressed arrays have $O(s^2)$ entries,
and all original-tree work is charged to the $|I|$-dependent term.
\end{proof}

\section{Relative compression with rounded conditional probabilities}
Consider $s=2k$ input occurrences and $M\leq d^2\leq4k^2$ nonempty
descriptors from Lemma~\ref{lem:oracle}. Let $p,\eta\in(0,1]$ be rational.
For a trace with $a$ input occurrences put $u=a/(2k)$. A sequence of $k$
uniform independent occurrence draws is bad for this trace if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bad}
 \left|\frac{c}{k}-u\right|>\eta(p+u),
\end{equation}
where $c$ is its output count. The tolerance uses the \emph{input sample}
frequency, not an unavailable original frequency.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:compression}
If $2M\exp(-k\eta^2p/3)\leq1/8$, a deterministic compression chooses $k$
occurrences so that no descriptor is bad. In addition to constructing the
source trees, it uses $O(k^3\polylog(n+2))$ word operations when $k<N$,
where $N=O(n)$ and all parameter numerators and denominators have
$O(\log(n+2))$ bits up to fixed factors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the mean of $k$ Bernoulli variables of mean $u$, the two-tail bound is
\[
 \Pr[|c/k-u|>x]\leq2\exp\left(-\frac{kx^2}{2u+2x/3}\right).
\]
Indeed $\log\mathbb E e^{\lambda(X-u)}\leq
u(e^\lambda-1-\lambda)\leq u\lambda^2/(2(1-\lambda/3))$ for
$0\leq\lambda<3$. Markov's inequality with
$\lambda=x/(u+x/3)$ gives the upper tail. The lower tail follows from
$e^{-\lambda}-1+\lambda\leq\lambda^2/2$, with denominator $2u$;
it is empty for $x>u$. Means zero and one are deterministic. Substituting
$x=\eta(p+u)$ and $2u+2x/3\leq(8/3)(p+u)$ gives the relaxed bound
$2e^{-k\eta^2p/3}$. Thus the initial expected number of bad descriptors
is at most $1/8$.

For $0\leq a\leq2k$, $0\leq h,c$ with $c+h\leq k$, let $q[a,h,c]$
be the exact probability of~\eqref{eq:bad} after $h$ further draws.
Its terminal value is the bad-event indicator and
\[
 q[a,h,c]=\frac{a q[a,h-1,c+1]+(2k-a)q[a,h-1,c]}{2k}.
\]
Choose a power of two $D\geq64Mk^2$ and construct the integer table
\begin{align}
 U[a,0,c]&=D\ind\{\text{\eqref{eq:bad} holds}\},\nonumber\\
 U[a,h,c]&=\left\lceil
 \frac{aU[a,h-1,c+1]+(2k-a)U[a,h-1,c]}{2k}\right\rceil.\label{eq:table}
\end{align}
Convex averaging and one rounding unit per level prove by induction that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round}
 q[a,h,c]\leq U[a,h,c]/D\leq q[a,h,c]+h/D.
\end{equation}
At a prefix with $h$ draws remaining, maintain count $c_F$ for each trace.
Choose an occurrence $x$ minimizing the integer score
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
 V_x=\sum_F U[a_F,h-1,c_F+\ind\{x\in F\}],
\end{equation}
breaking ties by original occurrence position, and update all counts.
Let $Q$ be the true sum of conditional bad probabilities. Averaged over
the $2k$ occurrences, the true next sums equal $Q$, counting multiplicities.
By~\eqref{eq:round} the mean next upper score divided by $D$ is at most
$Q+M(h-1)/D$. The minimum score is at most that mean, and bounds the true
chosen next sum from above. The total possible increase in true potential
is therefore at most
\[
 \sum_{h=1}^k\frac{M(h-1)}D
 =\frac{Mk(k-1)}{2D}\leq\frac1{128}.
\]
The terminal potential is the integer number of bad descriptors and is
less than $1/8+1/128<1$. It is zero. Exact monotonicity of the rounded
scores is unnecessary. If $M=0$, all occurrences are dummy and $k$ dummy
draws suffice without a table.

It remains to compute \emph{every} candidate score efficiently. At one draw
write $b_F=U[a_F,h-1,c_F]$,
$\delta_F=U[a_F,h-1,c_F+1]-b_F$, and $B=\sum_F b_F$.
Then $V_x=B+\sum_{F\ni x}\delta_F$. For each selected-source tree add
$\delta_{(u,v)}$ at target $v$ and sum accumulators from leaves to root.
The accumulator at $x$ is exactly the sum over descendant targets, hence
over root-to-target traces containing $x$. Sum these values over sources.
A real occurrence has this sum plus $B$; a dummy has score $B$. Differences
may be negative and are stored with signs. Repeated occurrences share a
score but retain their multiplicities in averaging and in the output.
All descriptors, traversals, candidates and Euler-membership count updates
take $O(k^2)$ operations per draw, hence $O(k^3)$ in total. The table also
has $O(k^3)$ entries, including all values of $a$.

For $k<N$ we have $M<4N^2$. Take the least power of two at least $64Mk^2$,
so $D<128Mk^2$ and $\log D=O(\log N)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:round} gives
$0\leq U\leq D+k$. Products in~\eqref{eq:table} and signed sums of
magnitude at most $O(M(D+k))$ use a constant number of words. In our
application $k$ is a power of two, so division by $2k$ is a shift; even
elementary polylogarithmic arithmetic suffices. All counters, table entries
and updates are charged, and no exact probability oracle or large-binomial
unit-cost operation is used.
\end{proof}

\section{A deterministically constructed stitching set}
For $n\geq2$, let $N$ be the least power of two at least $n$, padding with
$N-n$ dummy positions. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
 \begin{gathered}
 t=\min\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}:z^3\geq n^2\},\qquad
 L=\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil,\qquad H=L+1,\\
 p=t/N,\qquad \eta=1/(16H),\qquad
 k=2^{\lceil\log_2(512p^{-1}\eta^{-2}L)\rceil}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
These are integer/rational calculations: find $t$ by binary search and cube
comparisons, and $k$ by doubling and exact comparison. Thus
$t=\Theta(n^{2/3})$ and $k=O(n^{1/3}\log^3(n+2))$. Parameter calculations
use $O(\log(n+2))$ bits up to fixed constants. For these parameters the
terminal bad-event test in~\eqref{eq:table} is precisely
\[
 16HN|2c-a|>2kt+aN,
\]
so it too is an integer comparison of the stated bit length.

If $k\geq N$, use every real point as the stitching set and skip compression.
We charge this branch in Section~\ref{sec:cost}. Otherwise $k$ divides $N$.
Partition padded index order into consecutive $k$-position blocks, represented
initially by their exact occurrences. Merge adjacent equal-size \emph{original}
intervals, joining their $k$ representatives into $2k$ occurrences and
compressing to $k$ with the full original parent interval as background.
The children have both equal original sizes and equal representative sizes.

At every merge $M\leq4k^2<4N^2$ and $k\eta^2p\geq512L$. Since
$e^L>2^L\geq N+2$, we have
\[
 2M e^{-k\eta^2p/3}\leq8N^2e^{-(512/3)L}<1/8.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:compression} therefore applies uniformly, independently of
the representatives and their multiplicities. Lemmas~\ref{lem:oracle}
and~\ref{lem:compression} give per-merge cost
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nodecost}
 O\bigl(|I|k\log^2(n+2)+k^3\polylog(n+2)\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:net}
The construction returns a set $R$ of at most $k$ real points hitting every
canonical path of length at least $t$. On the branch $k<N$, its construction
cost is $O(Nk\log^3(n+2)+Nk^2\log^2(n+2))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix any $F\in\mathcal F$. At a node let $\phi$ be its frequency among
original padded positions and $r$ its representative frequency. After
$j$ compression levels we prove simultaneously for all $F$ that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant}
 |r-\phi|\leq E_j(\phi+p),\qquad E_j=(1+\eta)^j-1.
\end{equation}
The base blocks are exact. At a merge both original and representative
frequencies average their two children with equal weights. If $u$ is the
joined representative frequency, $|u-\phi|\leq E_j(\phi+p)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:consistency} and the full-background oracle cover its trace;
an empty trace stays exactly empty. Compression adds at most $\eta(p+u)$.
Since $p+u\leq(1+E_j)(p+\phi)$, the new error is at most
$[E_j+\eta(1+E_j)](\phi+p)=E_{j+1}(\phi+p)$. This proves the invariant,
including repeated representatives and dummy occurrences.

There are at most $H$ levels. Bounding binomial coefficients by $H^i$ gives
$(1+1/(16H))^H<\sum_{i\geq0}16^{-i}=16/15$, so $E_H<1/15$.
At the root, a path of length at least $t$ has $\phi\geq p$ and hence
$r\geq\phi-(\phi+p)/15\geq13\phi/15>0$. Discard dummies and deduplicate
only at the end. This preserves every hit and gives $|R|\leq k$.

For cost, parent original intervals are disjoint at each level, so the
background term of~\eqref{eq:nodecost} sums to $O(Nk\log^2(n+2))$ per
level and $O(Nk\log^3(n+2))$ overall. There are $N/k-1<N/k$ merge nodes.
The $k^3$ term sums to $O(Nk^2\log^2(n+2))$, where a squared logarithm
more than covers elementary arithmetic on a constant number of words.
Original source trees are processed one at a time. Working memory is
bounded by these time charges. In particular there is no global-$n$ scan
at every small merge and no enumeration of all global endpoint paths.
\end{proof}

Substituting~\eqref{eq:parameters} gives construction time
$O(n^{5/3}\log^8(n+2))$. The sample scale follows the established relative
sampling principle; the lemma supplies the additional original-background
implementation and its global accounting. The constants are conservative
and make no claim of practical efficiency.

\section{Reporting all lengths with additive output cost}
We give the details of the truncated-LIS and stitching integration to keep
the full reporting guarantee explicit. Their basic framework is from
\cite{KR26,KRfull}. All intermediate candidates will be feasible, and only
the final winner for a query is expanded.

\subsection{Short answers}
Build a balanced binary tree on the original index order. At each split
into children $I_L,I_R$, for $v\in I_L$ and $1\leq\alpha\leq t$ store
$A(v,\alpha)$, the minimum last value of an $\alpha$-chain starting at
$v$ within $I_L$. For $v\in I_R$ store $B(v,\beta)$, the maximum first
value of a $\beta$-chain ending at $v$ within $I_R$. Infeasible states
have separate flags, denoted $+\infty$ and $-\infty$ respectively.
Length-one states equal $a_v$. For larger lengths the recurrences are
\begin{align}
 A(v,\alpha)&=\min_{\substack{w>v,\ a_w>a_v\\w\in I_L}}
                    A(w,\alpha-1),\label{eq:shortA}\\
 B(v,\beta)&=\max_{\substack{w<v,\ a_w<a_v\\w\in I_R}}
                    B(w,\beta-1).\label{eq:shortB}
\end{align}
Separating the first or last element proves these formulas. Store an
attaining link to a previous-length state, giving an output-linear witness.
For each length, strict-rank Fenwick sweeps cost $O(|I|\log(n+2))$.
Summing over all splits and levels costs $O(nt\log^2(n+2))$ and stores
$O(nt\log(n+2))$ states; cap lengths by child size.

For each split and length, store feasible $A$ records in a static
three-dimensional range-min structure with coordinates
$(v,a_v,A(v,\alpha))$, weight $A(v,\alpha)$ and a witness handle.
Use range-max structures for $B$ records. Nested balanced range trees
provide $O(\log^3(n+2))$ query time and, conservatively,
$O(q\log^3(n+2))$ preprocessing on $q$ records: recursively decompose a
box into canonical subsets and store an extremal record at each final
subset. This gives $O(nt\log^4(n+2))$ total preprocessing, using only
coordinate comparisons and stored finite extrema.

Normalize query endpoints by comparison/binary search. Empty and
single-position queries are direct. For $\ell<r$ let the lowest index-tree
node containing both endpoints split at $s$, so $\ell\leq s<r$. For each
positive length up to $t$ obtain
\begin{align*}
 L_\alpha&=\min\{A(v,\alpha):\ell\leq v\leq s,\ a\leq a_v\leq b,
                                      \ A(v,\alpha)\leq b\},\\
 R_\beta&=\max\{B(v,\beta):s<v\leq r,\ a\leq a_v\leq b,
                                      \ B(v,\beta)\geq a\}.
\end{align*}
Every witness lies inside the query: internal indices and values lie
between its endpoints. Conversely any feasible left $\alpha$-chain has
last value at least the $A$ value of its start, and that minimizing witness
satisfies the same boundaries. Thus $L_\alpha$ is exactly the minimum
possible left peak. Similarly $R_\beta$ is the maximum possible right base.
Two positive witnesses concatenate precisely when $L_\alpha<R_\beta$;
query membership uses inclusive boundaries, but this join is strict.

Include zero-length empty states and every feasible one-sided chain.
The feasible $L_\alpha$ are nondecreasing with $\alpha$: drop the last
element of any longer chain. Similarly $R_\beta$ are nonincreasing, by
dropping the first element. Infeasible lengths form suffixes. For each
$\alpha\leq t$ binary search the largest compatible $\beta\leq t$ and
retain the maximum $\alpha+\beta$, also considering $\alpha=0$.
Every candidate is feasible. If the query optimum is at most $t$, its two
side lengths occur in this search and their extremal endpoints preserve
compatibility; the maximum candidate is therefore optimal. For a longer
optimum this routine still returns a feasible chain, possibly shorter.
Only two witness handles are retained. Its total overhead is bounded by
$O((n+m)t\log^6(n+m+2))$.

\subsection{Long answers and final witnesses}
For each $z\in R$, compute longest strict paths from $z$ to every reachable
northeast endpoint and from every reachable southwest endpoint to $z$.
Use strict-rank sweeps, reversed for backward paths, storing lengths and
parent links. Any attaining deterministic tie rule suffices here; these
trees need not be the canonical sampling trees. The cost is
$O(n\log^2(n+2))$ per $z$. Put the endpoint coordinates, lengths and
handles in static two-dimensional range-max structures.

For a query containing $z$, maximize forward and backward lengths over
endpoints in the query. Both paths lie in the query by endpoint-box
containment, and strict index order makes their only common vertex $z$.
Their concatenation is feasible and has length equal to the two lengths
minus one. If $z$ lies on a canonical optimum, the maximized halves are
at least its corresponding halves. Their feasible concatenation must
therefore be optimal. Lemma~\ref{lem:net} supplies such a $z$ whenever the
optimum is at least $t$; the short routine handles optima at most $t$.
Take the largest feasible candidate, including the empty one. Threshold
equality causes no gap. The stitching overhead is
$O((n+m)|R|\log^3(n+m+2))$.

Keep only lengths and constant-size candidate handles during comparison.
Finally expand one winner per query. A stitching witness follows its two
parent trees, reverses one list in an output-sized buffer, and suppresses
the duplicated $z$. A short witness follows its stored length-state links.
Expansion takes $O(K)$ overall; losing candidates are never expanded.

\section{Total cost, uniformity and limitations}\label{sec:cost}
On the branch $k<N$, the charged overhead is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total}
 \begin{split}
 O\bigl(& (n+m)t\log^6(n+m+2)+Nk\log^3(n+2)\\
        &+Nk^2\log^2(n+2)+(n+m)k\log^3(n+m+2)\bigr).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
With $t=\Theta(n^{2/3})$, $N<2n$ and
$k=O(n^{1/3}\log^3(n+2))$, this is within Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
In particular, $O(n^{5/3}\log^8(n+2))$ construction is absorbed by the
$(n+m)n^{2/3}$ overhead with its stated logarithms.

On the fallback branch $k\geq N$, take $R$ to be all real points and build
no compression tables. All-point stitching costs
$O((n+m)n\log^3(n+m+2))$. The branch condition gives $n\leq k$, and
the formula for $k$ still gives $k=O(n^{1/3}\log^3(n+2))$ on this branch.
Thus the same theorem bounds this fallback, without an uncharged finite
threshold. The conservative constants can make the fallback apply over a
very large finite range. For $n=1$ direct comparison costs $O(m+1+K)$;
if $m=0$ return immediately.

All sizes, addresses, counters, ranks and finite integer intermediates are
polynomial in $n+m$. A fixed number of words accommodates constant-factor
larger bit lengths, and the arithmetic charges in Lemma~\ref{lem:compression}
are included in~\eqref{eq:total}. Original real values are only compared
and stored. Equal values have equal ranks and never follow each other.
Open or closed query boundaries are normalized by the corresponding
comparison searches. The simultaneous hitting property is independent of
the number, overlap or density of supplied queries. This completes the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in the full reporting model.

Matching the randomized square-root overhead deterministically is a natural
next quantitative goal. Extending the original-interval oracle to other range
optimization problems is another direction.
