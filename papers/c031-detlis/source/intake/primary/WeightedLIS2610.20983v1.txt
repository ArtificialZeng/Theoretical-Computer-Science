                                                                                      Abstract
                                                  We give a deterministic data structure for range weighted longest increasing subse-
                                               quence (LIS) queries on n values with positive weights. Preprocessing takes O(n log2 n)
                                               time and space. Value queries take O(log n) time, and an optimal subsequence of k
                                               elements can be reported in O(k + log n) time. This resolves an open question on truly
                                               subquadratic preprocessing with polylogarithmic query time. The key is a single-crossing
                                               property of envelopes for value-separated endpoint sets, enabling O(log n)-time persistent
                                               merges. The bounds assume constant-time exact addition and comparison of weights.


                                         1     Introduction
                                         Let a0 , . . . , an−1 be a sequence with positive real weights w0 , . . . , wn−1 . A range weighted
                                         longest increasing subsequence query asks for the maximum total weight of a strictly increasing
                                         subsequence in an index interval [l, r). A reporting query also returns an optimal subsequence.
                                             For unweighted range LIS, Tiskin’s semi-local comparison techniques give O(n log2 n)
                                         preprocessing and O(log n) time to return the length [Tis15]; see also the formulation
                                         in [GGK25, Lemma B.14]. Gawrychowski, Gorbachev, and Kociumaka [GGK25, Theorem 1.3]
                                         support reporting in O(k logα n) time after O(n log3−α n) preprocessing, for every α ∈ [0, 1].
                                         Here k is the output length. Their construction uses core-sparse Monge matrix multiplication,
                                         where the core records nonzero mixed second differences. The theorem and appendix numbers
                                         cited from [GGK25] refer to its full version.
                                             For arbitrary positive weights, Gawrychowski et al. [GGK25, Appendix B.1] ask whether
                                         truly subquadratic preprocessing and polylogarithmic query time are possible. Their
                                         alignment-matrix construction remains correct, but the unweighted core-size guarantee
                                         no longer follows from their analysis. Karthik C. S. and Rahul [KR26, Section 7 of the full
                                         version] also ask for a truly subquadratic algorithm for weighted one-dimensional range LIS.

                                         1.1    Our result
                                         We answer these questions without bounding the core size.

                                         Theorem 1.1. There is a deterministic data structure for range weighted LIS with O(n log2 n)
                                         preprocessing time and space, O(log n) value-query time, and O(k + log n) reporting time.

                                              The query intervals need not be known during preprocessing. The algorithm is exact, and
                                         its time and space bounds are independent of the weight magnitudes. We count exact additions
                                         and comparisons of weights, comparisons of input values, and operations on O(log n)-bit
                                         indices and pointers at unit cost. Space counts real-weight cells and integer words.

1.2   Overview
For each endpoint p, let Dp (l) be the maximum weight of an increasing subsequence ending
at p with all indices at least l, or zero if l > p. For an endpoint set E, let HE be the pointwise
maximum of zero and its endpoint functions. The optimum for [l, r) is H[0,r) (l).
    The key is a single-crossing property. If all endpoint values in L are smaller than those in
R, then HR (l) > HL (l) holds exactly on a prefix of left boundaries. We derive this from a
pairwise property using a planar path-exchange argument [Sch98, GGK25]. Consequently,
HL∪R consists of a prefix of HR and a suffix of HL .
    We represent each envelope by a persistent segment tree indexed by l. Single crossing
permits O(log n)-time merges with O(log n) new nodes. A mutable segment tree over input
values stores these roots. A value-prefix query obtains the predecessor envelope, and a
persistent prefix update constructs the new endpoint function. Updating the outer tree takes
O(log n) further merges. Thus each element costs O(log2 n) time and space. Saving the
envelope root stored at the outer root after each input prefix reduces a range query to one
point evaluation.
    For reporting, persistent nodes store shared suffix witnesses represented by binary con-
catenations. Their weights serve as additive lazy tags. A query collects these suffixes along
one tree path in O(log n) time. Expanding them takes O(k) time. The construction requires
no bound on Monge core size.

3    Single-crossing property
We show that, when two endpoint sets are separated by value, the higher-value envelope
exceeds the lower-value envelope exactly on a prefix of left boundaries. Ties favour the
lower-value set. The proof uses the standard planar path-exchange argument underlying
Monge inequalities [GGK25, Fact B.7].
Lemma 3.1. Let p, q ∈ [0, n) satisfy ap < aq . Then there exists c ∈ [0, n] such that

                            {l ∈ [0, n) | Dp (l) < Dq (l)} = [0, c).

Proof. If p < q, any increasing subsequence ending at p can be extended by q. For l ≤ p,

                                Dq (l) ≥ Dp (l) + wq > Dp (l).

For p < l ≤ q,
                                  Dq (l) ≥ wq > 0 = Dp (l),
while both functions vanish for l > q. Thus the set is [0, q + 1).
   Suppose instead that q < p. We first show, for 0 ≤ l < l′ ≤ q, that

                              Dp (l) + Dq (l′ ) ≤ Dq (l) + Dp (l′ ).                       (5)

Use the standard alignment-grid representation [Sch98, GGK25], with positive weights on
the diagonal edges. Consider the directed grid on [0, n]2 with east and north edges of weight
zero. For every input position i, add the diagonal edge

                          (i, ai ) −→ (i + 1, ai + 1)    of weight wi .

The resulting graph is planar: diagonals lie inside distinct unit squares and intersect grid
edges only at their endpoints.
   Put sl = (l, 0) and ti = (i + 1, ai + 1). For l ≤ i, the diagonal edges of any sl –ti path
correspond to an increasing subsequence in [l, i] whose values are at most ai . Conversely,
every such subsequence determines a path of the same weight by joining its diagonal edges

with zero-weight grid paths. If such a subsequence omits i, its indices and values are smaller
than those of i. Appending i increases its weight. Hence the maximum path weight from sl
to ti is exactly Di (l).
    Choose maximum-weight paths from sl to tp and from sl′ to tq . Both lie in [l, p + 1] ×
[0, aq + 1]. The four terminals sl , sl′ , tp , tq are distinct and occur in this cyclic order on
the boundary of the rectangle. Thus the two paths have a common vertex by planarity; see
Figure 1. Swapping their suffixes at that vertex gives paths from sl to tq and from sl′ to tp .
The sum of their weights is unchanged. Since their weights are at most Dq (l) and Dp (l′ ),
respectively, (5) follows.
    By (5), Dq (l) − Dp (l) is nonincreasing on [0, q]. For l > q, Dq (l) = 0 ≤ Dp (l). Hence the
set in the lemma is a prefix of [0, n).

    The pairwise property extends to value-separated endpoint sets.

Lemma 3.2. Let L, R ⊆ [0, n) satisfy ap < aq for all p ∈ L and q ∈ R. Then there exists
c ∈ [0, n] such that HL (l) < HR (l) if and only if l < c, for every l ∈ [0, n). Consequently,
                                                (
                                                    HR (l),   l < c,
                                  HL∪R (l) =                                                   (6)
                                                    HL (l),   l ≥ c.

Proof. Suppose HL (l0 ) ≥ HR (l0 ). If HL (l0 ) = 0, both envelopes remain zero for l ≥ l0 .
   Otherwise, choose p ∈ L attaining HL (l0 ). For every q ∈ R,

                                  Dq (l0 ) ≤ HR (l0 ) ≤ Dp (l0 ).

By Lemma 3.1, Dq (l) ≤ Dp (l) for all l ≥ l0 . Taking the maximum over q and zero gives

                             HR (l) ≤ Dp (l) ≤ HL (l)           (l ≥ l0 ).

Hence the strict comparison holds on a prefix. Choosing HL at equality gives (6).


4     Persistent envelope representations
We represent envelopes and their witnesses in persistent segment trees. All nodes are
immutable; updates use path copying [DSST89]. Beyond persistent range updates, we only
need to merge envelope pairs satisfying the single-crossing property.

4.1   Witnesses and suffix tags
A witness is an index sequence represented by an immutable handle: the empty sequence
ε, a singleton Leaf(i), or a binary concatenation Cat(U, V ). We set Cat(ε, V ) = V and
Cat(U, ε) = U ; otherwise, concatenation creates one node pointing to the two nonempty
handles. Each handle stores its total weight W , where

           W (ε) = 0,      W (Leaf(i)) = wi ,           W (Cat(U, V )) = W (U ) + W (V ).

Thus concatenation takes O(1) time and space, without copying or balancing the represented
sequences. Binary reconstruction witnesses of this form also appear in [GGK25, Appendix B,
proof of Theorem 1.3]. These operations apply to arbitrary index sequences. The feasibility
of the constructed witnesses is proved in Section 5.
    All function trees use the same balanced binary subdivision of [0, n). An interval [b, e) of
length at least two splits at m = ⌊(b + e)/2⌋. There is one leaf per coordinate, and the height

Basic operations. The operation Append(T, V ) appends V at every coordinate of T . It
copies the root, replaces its suffix by Cat(S, V ), and increases its cache by W (V ). It takes
O(1) time and creates at most one function node and one witness node.
    For a nonleaf root, Children(T ) returns its two restrictions by applying Append to each
child with the parent’s suffix. The order is child suffix followed by parent suffix, as in (7).
Conversely, Join(f, g) creates a parent with children f, g, empty suffix, and cache last(g). Both
operations take O(1) time and create O(1) nodes. They leave all existing nodes unchanged.

4.2   Prefix updates
For c ∈ [0, n], let PrefixAppend(T, c, V ) append V at coordinates below c and leave all other
coordinates unchanged. This is an ordinary persistent prefix update, given in Algorithm 1.
On a fully covered interval it calls Append; on an uncovered interval it returns the original
root. Only the interval containing the boundary is partially covered at any level. Each level
performs O(1) operations, so the update takes O(log n) time and creates O(log n) function
and witness nodes.

single prefix update:

4.3    Merging envelopes
Lemma 4.1. Let f and g be represented on the same interval [b, e). Suppose g(l) > f (l)
holds exactly on a prefix of this interval. Their pointwise maximum can be represented in
O(log n) time using O(log n) new function and witness nodes. At equality, the witness of f is
chosen. Both input trees remain unchanged.

Proof. Consider Algorithm 2. At a leaf, the strict comparison selects the larger score,
preferring f at equality. Otherwise, Children incorporates the parent suffixes into the child
roots. If last(gL ) > last(fL ), the prefix property implies that g exceeds f throughout the left
half. Otherwise, g does not exceed f anywhere in the right half. Thus one half is selected
directly, and only the other requires recursion. Restriction preserves the prefix property,
proving correctness by induction.
    Each call makes at most one recursive call and otherwise takes O(1) time and creates
O(1) new function and witness nodes. The tree height gives the claimed bounds.

    Cached scores allow Merge to construct the pointwise maximum in a single descent. By
Lemma 3.2, Merge(HL , HR ) is applicable whenever the endpoint sets are separated in this
value order.

4.4    Evaluation and reporting
Lemma 4.2. A represented score at a given coordinate can be returned in O(log n) time,
and its witness of length k can be reported in O(k + log n) time. Neither query changes the
stored structure.

Proof. Descend to the coordinate’s leaf, collecting the suffixes S0 , . . . , Sh in root-to-leaf order.
By (7), the score is hj=0 W (Sj ), which is computed in O(log n) time. The witness is the

the usual balanced recursive subdivision, so no padding is needed.
Lemma 5.1. Algorithm 3 constructs root[r] = H[0,r) for every r ∈ [0, n], with optimum
witnesses for positive values and empty witnesses for zero values. Its time and total allocated
space are O(n log2 n).
Proof. We prove (9) and its witness assertion by induction on i. They hold initially. The
canonical intervals partition [0, ai ). Their ordered merges yield Pi by Lemmas 3.2 and 4.1.
For l ≤ i, a predecessor witness of positive weight ends at an earlier position of smaller value,
and appending i gives a feasible subsequence of weight Pi (l) + wi . A predecessor witness of
weight zero is empty and gives the singleton i. For l > i, the update leaves the zero value
and empty witness unchanged. Thus (4) proves that the new root represents Di with the
required witnesses.
    Since the ranks are distinct, the leaf for ai was previously empty. At each updated
ancestor, the child endpoint sets are separated by value. They partition the set in (9) with i
replaced by i + 1. Their merge preserves the invariant and selects an optimum witness. All
other outer nodes are unchanged. The outer root consequently represents H[0,i+1) . Persistence
preserves each saved root and its witnesses.
    Each iteration uses O(log n) merges and one prefix append. By Lemma 4.1 and the
prefix-update bound, it takes O(log2 n) time and allocates O(log2 n) function and witness
nodes. Relabeling takes O(n log n) time, and building the initial trees takes O(n) time and
space. The input, mutable outer tree, and saved roots occupy O(n) space. Every allocated
node has constant size, so even retaining all temporary nodes uses O(n log2 n) space. The
recursion stack uses O(log n) additional space.
