## Primary results checked

1. Fillmore, Goeckner, Kirsch, Martin and McGinnis, *The existence and
   structure of universal partial cycles*, Designs, Codes and Cryptography
   94, article 152 (published 4 July 2026),
   [publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-026-01841-x),
   DOI `10.1007/s10623-026-01841-x`.
   Section 2's definitions count cyclic starting indices and exclude the
   all-wildcard and ordinary De Bruijn degeneracies. Theorem 3.1 supplies
   binary n=8 examples with d=1. Corollary 5.2 retains n and d while
   multiplying alphabet size. Remark 6.6 and Question 9.2 explicitly leave
   d>1 existence open. Corollary 8.14 restricts period-four frames to d=n/4;
   it supplies no existence construction. Thus n=12,d=3 is consistent with
   the restrictions, but is not furnished by them. The paragraph after
   Question 9.2 also raises the regime n not dividing a^(n-d).

2. [arXiv:2310.13067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13067): the current record
   identifies v3, revised 14 April 2025, as its latest revision. The later
   publisher full text above controls the present comparison.

3. Carey, Kearney, Kirsch and Popescu, *Universal partial tori*, Designs,
   Codes and Cryptography 93, 2803–2822 (published 27 March 2025),
   [publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-025-01609-9),
   DOI `10.1007/s10623-025-01609-9`.
   Definition 8.2 permits several component cycles in a universal partial
   quasi-family. Section 9 says the known upcycles and quasi-families have
   diamondicity one. This distinguishes prior multi-cycle objects from the
   candidate's directly verified single cyclic word.

4. [Rachel Kirsch's current publication/preprint list](https://mason.gmu.edu/~rkirsch4/)
   was opened. It lists the 2026 cycle paper and 2025 tori paper and does
   not list a further d>1 resolution. This is an author-maintained pointer,
   not an exhaustive priority registry.

5. A fresh search also surfaced the
   [KolKom 2026 program](https://kolkom26.zib.de/). Its occurrence of the
   cycle paper is a reference in a talk about shorthand permutation tori
   (joint work associated with arXiv:2609.27583). The inspected abstract
   concerns a different object and does not give a d>1 upcycle result.
